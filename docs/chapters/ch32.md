# §16 — Chapter 32: Anesthetic Implications of Concurrent Diseases

> Chapter ledger entry, relocated from `goldenversion.md`. Historical record — read only if
> you need this chapter's detailed rationale.

**Content classification.** Ch32 (Kiefer, Mythen, Roizen, Fleisher) is, like Ch31, almost entirely **Bucket B**, but its content is broader and more **cross-cutting** across phase of care (PreOp risk-stratification, Intraoperative drug/monitoring choices, and some PostOp/ICU content for crises like DKA and refeeding syndrome). The simulator already had basic comorbidity-specific guidance for diabetes, COPD, and CAD/CHF (built during the Ch24-30 sessions); this chapter's value-add is the comorbidities and decision tools that were genuinely missing: OSA risk stratification, chronic antihypertensive/statin continuation rules, and pheochromocytoma adrenergic-blockade management. No Bucket C content is present.

#### 16.1 Obstructive Sleep Apnea: STOP-BANG Risk Score (`calculateStopBangScore`, `STOP_BANG_ITEMS`)

Ch32's KEY POINTS flag OSA as clinically critical — "increased sensitivity to... the depressing effects of hypnotics and opioids on airway muscle tone and respiration, as well as the difficulty with laryngoscopy and mask ventilation" — but the chapter does not itself name a scoring instrument. The standard published **STOP-BANG** tool (Chung et al.) is added as a disclosed best-reasoned addition (general medical knowledge, not a chapter-table transcription) to give this KEY POINT an actual, scorable surface:

*   **`STOP_BANG_ITEMS`** / **`calculateStopBangScore(answers)`**: the 8 standard items, scored 0-2 (low) / 3-4 (intermediate) / 5-8 (high) risk.
*   Surfaced in `PreOpEMR.jsx`'s Risk Assessment tab as a third supplementary tool (alongside Ch30's RCRI quiz and Ch31's DASI calculator). Four items (BMI>35, Age>50, male sex, HTN as a proxy for "Pressure") are **auto-derived** from the existing patient chart; the remaining four (snoring, tiredness, observed apnea, neck circumference) are answered by the user, mirroring the real preoperative interview.
*   The computed score/risk-level is persisted onto `patient.osaStopBangScore`/`osaRiskLevel` at case-start (`handleProceed()`), the same pattern Ch31's RCRI/anticoagulation data already uses — so it is available to the rest of the simulator (Attending chat, advisories, quality events) for the lifetime of the case.
*   **New advisories** (`getAnesthesiaAdvisories()`): a high STOP-BANG score with Direct Laryngoscopy planned now warns about anticipated difficult mask ventilation/laryngoscopy; a high score with General Anesthesia and no BIS/quantitative-NMB monitoring selected now surfaces an info-level advisory recommending both, citing the chapter's own opioid/hypnotic-sensitivity rationale.
*   **New `QualityEvent`**: locking a GA plan on a high-OSA-risk patient without BIS or quantitative NMB monitoring emits a `moderate`-severity, `Monitoring`-category, `PreOp`-phase event (`chapterSource: 'Ch32... Obstructive Sleep Apnea'`) — not a hard block, consistent with this codebase's established "score, don't always prevent" pattern for real-but-non-immediately-catastrophic choices.
*   **Attending chat**: a new query handler (`ClinicalAiChat.js`) answers OSA/STOP-BANG questions grounded in the live `patient.osaStopBangScore`/`osaRiskLevel`, falling back to a prompt to complete the tool if it hasn't been run yet for the active case.

#### 16.2 Chronic Cardiovascular Medication Management (`calculateChronicMedicationManagementPlan`)

Directly extends the Ch31 "Perioperative Medication Management" card with chronic, non-anticoagulant cardiovascular medications — kept as a separate exported function so Ch31's existing behavior/tests are untouched:

*   **Antihypertensives**: Ch32 KEY POINTS, directly sourced — continue ALL antihypertensive classes morning-of-surgery EXCEPT ACE inhibitors and ARBs (held due to refractory intraoperative hypotension risk after induction). The function emits both a "hold ACE-I/ARB" and a "continue all others" entry when `patient.htn` is true; a disclosed addendum (not directly chapter-sourced, flagged as such in the rationale text) notes the additional general-knowledge concern about centrally-acting-agent withdrawal.
*   **Statins**: Ch32, directly sourced — continue statin therapy perioperatively without interruption if already prescribed. Indicated (and thus recommended for continuation) for documented CAD, diabetes age 40-75, or LDL ≥190 mg/dL — the function checks `patient.cad`, `patient.diabetes` + age band, and an optional `patient.ldl` field.
*   Rendered as a new "Chronic Cardiovascular Medication Management (Ch32)" block in `PreOpEMR.jsx`'s Medications & Allergies card, beneath the existing Ch31 anticoagulation block.

#### 16.3 Diabetes: Refined Glycemic Target & Autonomic Neuropathy Note

The existing hardcoded Endocrinology consult note (`generatePreOpResults()`, present since an earlier chapter session) targeted intraoperative glucose at a flat "140-180 mg/dL" with no cited source. Ch32 cites the **WHO Surgical Safety Checklist bundle** target explicitly: 100-180 mg/dL (6-10 mmol/L), acceptable range 70-215 mg/dL (4-12 mmol/L) — tighter control is explicitly *not* recommended given the NICE-SUGAR-trial-documented dose-response relationship between hypoglycemia severity and mortality. The consult note was updated to cite this target and to add a line on diabetic autonomic neuropathy screening (sinus arrhythmia <5 bpm on deep inspiration is associated with painless MI and cardiorespiratory arrest — directly sourced) with the chapter's specific countermeasure (metoclopramide 10mg IV preop for gastroparesis-related aspiration risk).

#### 16.4 Pheochromocytoma: Adrenergic Blockade Adequacy (`assessPheoBlockadeAdequacy`) — Groundwork Only

Ch32 gives an unusually complete, directly-sourced protocol: alpha-adrenergic blockade (phenoxybenzamine 20-30 mg/70kg PO once or twice daily, titrated to 60-250 mg/day over at least 7-14 days, typically 2-6 weeks) must be established *before* any beta-adrenergic blockade (propranolol, added only for persistent tachycardia/arrhythmia) — beta-blockade first risks unopposed alpha-vasoconstriction and malignant hypertension. The chapter also gives four explicit adequacy-of-blockade criteria.

*   **`assessPheoBlockadeAdequacy(criteria)`**: implements the four criteria (no in-hospital BP >165/90 for 48h preop; standing BP not below 80/45; ECG free of non-permanent ST-T changes; ≤1 PVC per 5 minutes) as a pure, exported, unit-tested function.
*   **No case preset in this simulator currently models a pheochromocytoma patient** — there is no `patient.pheo` flag or equivalent to score this function against live state. This is built and tested as a reusable function for a future case rather than wired into a currently-playable feature.
*   **Attending chat**: a new query handler answers "how do I prepare a pheochromocytoma patient" with the full alpha-before-beta protocol, drug names/doses, and adequacy criteria as general reference knowledge — satisfying the requirement that Bucket B content be surfaced at runtime somewhere, even when it isn't yet patient-specific.

#### 16.5 What Was Not Integrated This Session

Ch32 is a 62-page chapter covering many additional comorbidities. **Not integrated** (left as narrative source material, not fabricated into simulator state without a corresponding patient flag): diabetic ketoacidosis/hyperglycemic crisis management (anion gap, fluid resuscitation rates, phosphorus replacement — Bucket A-adjacent but no DKA case/flag exists), the diabetes "accelerated physiologic aging" multipliers (1.75/1.5/1.25/1.06 years per chronologic year — a real, citable risk-communication concept, but with no obvious live-state hook beyond informational chat content, which it wasn't added to this session for time), anorexia nervosa/bulimia/starvation metabolic derangements and critical thresholds (BMI<13, WBC<3.0×10⁹/L), adrenal incidentaloma prevalence statistics, TPN/refeeding-syndrome management (serum albumin as an outcome predictor, hypophosphatemia thresholds), thyroid disease hormone-binding-ratio interpretation, spinal cord injury/autonomic hyperreflexia management, and detailed pulmonary-disease and renal-disease risk checklists beyond what RCRI/GFR-staging already cover. None of these map to an existing patient flag or case type, so adding them now would have meant inventing patient state rather than wiring real chapter content to real simulator data.
